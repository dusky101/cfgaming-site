# 02.4: Charddie Games' banner

Written on 14 November 2026. Agree it with Marc before it starts.

## Why, in Marc's words

"if there is a way to use this image to promote my kids youtube channel at the bottom of the webpage or something… maybe in the about section?"

## The picture

- **The file:** `CharddieGStrip.png`, from Marc's Preview documents in iCloud. 2034 × 468 pixels, 1.2 MB, no transparency.
- **What it shows:**
  - the two cartoon gamers from CF Gaming's logo, one with a football and one with a toy blaster;
  - the words "Charddie Games" and "Let's Play";
  - a blurred stadium on the left, and a blurred keyboard and gamepad on the right.
- **Looked at against `.claude/rules/site-content.md`** (by eye, at full size and in close-ups):
  - no console maker's logo or name. The gamepad is generic and blurred, with no button symbols that can be read.
  - nothing private: no real names or faces. The cartoon pair and the channel's name are already on the site, in the logo, About and the footer.
  - **Not known yet:** who made it, and with what. The sparkles in the background look like an AI image tool's, which is inferred, not proved. `CREDITS.md` needs its source, and if it was made with a tool, that tool's terms are read before it goes on a public site.

## The proposal

- **On About, as a section of its own** after "It began at home":
  - its heading, "Charddie Games";
  - the banner across the top of the card, with rounded corners;
  - the line already on the page, moved here: "CF Gaming's logo is the one I made for their YouTube channel", reworded to stand on its own;
  - a glass button, **Charddie Games on YouTube**, linking to the channel. No YouTube logo, and no embed.
- **The footer stays as it is:** its line "The logo comes from Charddie Games, on YouTube", on every page. The banner on every page would repeat a heavy picture under every page's download.
- **The picture, made by Astro:**
  - copied to `src/assets/brand/charddie-games-banner.png`;
  - shown with `<Picture>` as AVIF and WebP, at the widths About needs (up to twice its 46rem column);
  - with its width and height, so nothing shifts, and loaded lazily;
  - with alternative text that says what it shows, the words in it included.
- **At a phone's width** the strip is only about 80 pixels tall. If its words don't read there, phones show a crop of the gamers and the name.
- **`CREDITS.md`:** the banner, its source and its date.
- **Built on `preview`,** and published with phase 04's pictures (its step 9), on Marc's say.

## Marc's answers (14 November)

- **Where:** "About only".
- **Who made it:** "it was made with Canva after getting gemini to create the subjects of the image", on a Canva Pro account.
- **CF Gaming's logo:** Gemini drew all of it, so Canva's rule on logos (below) doesn't touch it.

## What the terms say (read 14 November)

- **Canva's Content License Agreement** (the page gives no date):
  - allowed for "advertising and promotional projects" and "web pages", on Free and Pro, with no credit needed outside editorial use;
  - Pro content: 480,000 pixels at most "per Content file where un-edited";
  - never as a file people can download, and never "as part of a trade-mark, design-mark, trade-name";
  - an Education account may use Pro content only for non-commercial work. Marc's is Pro.
- **Canva's AI Product Terms** (effective 26 June 2026): you own what Canva's AI makes, unless it changes Canva's own library pictures, and may use it "for any lawful purpose".
- **Google's Terms of Service** (effective 30 July 2026): Google "won't claim ownership" of what its services generate, and no credit is asked for. Its Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy bars passing generated content off as made only by a person, to deceive. So About and `CREDITS.md` both name Gemini.
- **Not read:** Canva's general Terms of Use, and Google's terms for Gemini itself.
